# September 25 — compact excavator lowered into the shaft field

All five iPhone HEIC originals in the supplied folder were inspected, copied unchanged, hashed and retained. Browser-compatible JPEG viewing copies were generated separately. Camera timestamps place the sequence between 6:59 a.m. and 12:22 p.m.; the camera timezone is not independently established.

## Evidence

- `IMG_0061.heic` is the broad dawn overview: excavation has exposed many drilled-shaft heads, rock-rich material and localized standing water.
- `IMG_0062.heic` and `IMG_0063.heic`, nine seconds apart, document one placement sequence. A large CAT excavator is lowering a compact CAT on a support platform into the deeper working pocket while workers monitor the operation. The observer directly watched the lowering.
- `IMG_0064.heic` restores the wider equipment context immediately after the paired frames.
- `IMG_0065.heic` is the clearest close landscape view of the compact machine working among exposed shaft heads. The large excavator remains above; workers and the orange access ladder provide scale.

The observer reports that the compact excavator performs finer excavation and cleaning around the shaft heads, and that steel tubing continues to be removed. The equipment roles are consistent with the visible access constraints, but the photographs do not establish lift depth, rigging design, production rate or final preparation criteria.

## Editorial limits

Use **drilled-shaft heads / foundation shafts**, not building columns. The documented sequence supports removal of exposed upper casing across the site. It does not prove that all casing has already been removed or that no casing remains below the visible excavation. The ladder, workers and machines establish qualitative scale only; no excavation depth should be calculated from perspective photographs.
