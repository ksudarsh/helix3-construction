## Evidence and limits

Observed: separated and mechanically handled casing shells; a casing split longitudinally into removable halves; substantially deeper excavation around drilled-shaft heads; projecting reinforcement; a broad zone of coarse angular material; workers providing scale beside a cased shaft.

Likely: the coarse material may be weathered/broken rock, rock fill or another rocky stratum. Rough concrete may reflect casting against an irregular bore or later shaft-head preparation.

Unknown: intact bedrock, rock type, excavation depth, concrete acceptance, retained lower-casing function and the final cap/mat/grade-beam arrangement.

The observer directly saw casing flattened, loaded and hauled away for recycling. The photographs document separation and mechanical handling; the recycling destination remains observer evidence rather than something visible in-frame.

The thermal cut in `IMG_0056.jpeg` is visible, and prior observer evidence establishes gas cutting. The specific fuel gas is not identifiable from the image. Cutting prevents intact casing reuse, but the upper casing performed an essential temporary stabilization role and would interfere with later structural connections if left at the shaft head. Recycling recovers the steel but not all embodied energy.
